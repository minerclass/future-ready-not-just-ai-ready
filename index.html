IL Future Ready Student Summit · October 6, 2026

Future Ready, Not Just AI Ready

Screen time, pedagogical friction, and the decisions only district leaders can make.

Micah Miner · Director of Innovation and Technology, Beach Park CCSD 3

Before we start

Two questions.

Talk to the people at your table. I want honest answers, not impressive ones.

Question one

Name one thing that got easier in your district this year because of AI.

Question two

Now name one thing your students got better at because of AI.

The gap

Those are not the same question.

The first one is about adults and workload. The second one is about students and learning. Most districts have been answering the first while believing they answered the second.

The distinction

Procurement question

AI ready

Do we have the tools, the policy, the training, the data agreements? Necessary work. Answerable in a year.

Graduate question

Future ready

Can our students think, judge, and make themselves understood when the tool is not there? Answerable in a decade.

What future ready means

The durable six

The capacities AI should amplify rather than absorb.

Critical thinking

Communication

Creativity

Collaboration

Judgment

Learner agency

Show of hands

Two questions about your Portrait of a Graduate.

  1. Who has one?
  2. Who has mapped their AI guidance against it?Not their acceptable use policy. Their guidance about what AI should and should not do for a student.

That second gap is what the rest of this hour is about.

First, a measurement problem

Screen time is the wrong question.

Every district I know has argued about minutes. Almost none have argued about what the minutes were doing.

Why the metric fails

Two students. Forty-five minutes each.

Student A

Read four conflicting sources, argued with a partner, threw out a first draft, wrote something she could defend.

Student B

Typed the prompt into a chatbot, skimmed the output, changed three words, submitted.

Your dashboard reports these identically. Same device, same minutes, same completion. One of them learned something.

The better question

Not how long the screen was on. What the screen made easy.

The reframe

Friction is not one thing.

Pedagogical friction is the productive resistance learning requires. The work, slowness, and struggle that make development possible. It is not a single quantity to minimize, and it is not an argument for making school harder.

It comes in layers, and only one of them is the layer technology should be reducing.

Reduce this layer

Infrastructural friction

The conditions that enable or constrain learning. Time, access, technology, policy, physical environment.

A student who cannot reach an archive. A family who cannot read the notice. A teacher who loses an evening to scheduling. These are barriers to learning, not the learning itself. Removing them expands who gets to do the work. This is where educational technology has always earned its place.
Protect this layer

Noetic friction

The cognitive work of thinking, reasoning, and knowing. Forming ideas, holding contradictions, reaching understanding.

When a tool does this work, the answer still appears. The thinking moved from the learner to the system.
Protect this layer

Rhetorical friction

The work of articulation. Making thought communicable through language, structure, and audience awareness.

Writing is not the transcription of a finished thought. Writing is how the thought gets finished. Fluent prose the student did not construct skips the part where the understanding forms.
Protect this layer

Existential friction

The work of becoming. The identity formation that happens through sustained engagement with difficulty.

A student who has never finished something hard does not know she can. That knowledge is not information. It is formation, and it only comes from the struggle.

The diagnostic

Unproductive success

The output looks right. The learning did not happen.

This is what makes the problem so hard to govern. Nothing looks broken. Grades hold. Completion rises. The dashboard is green. The formation that should have occurred did not, and it will be two years before it shows up in a writing sample.

The part that is nobody's fault

A tool cannot tell these layers apart.

Reducing friction is what the category exists to do. Resistance registers to a product as a problem to solve, not as a dimension with a purpose. That is structural, not a defect in anyone's software.

Which means the discernment can only live with the adults. No procurement process, no setting, and no vendor assurance moves that responsibility off your desk.

The lens

Four questions. Any tool, any vendor.

  1. What does this make easier?Be specific. "Saves teachers time" is not an answer.
  2. Which friction is it reducing?Infrastructural, or noetic, rhetorical, or existential?
  3. What is the student no longer doing, and was that the standard?If the removed step was the objective, the tool did not help with the objective.
  4. What signal would tell us it went wrong, and who is watching it?Most districts have no answer here at all.

At your table · four minutes

Run one of your own tools through it.

Something already in your buildings. Something you renewed without much discussion. Take it through all four questions and see where you get stuck.

Most tables get stuck on number four.

Before the examples, some disclosure

Why I am using our hosts as the worked example.

  • 1.They are hosting and I am being paid to be here. You should know that before I show you anything.
  • 2.Their products were in my district before I was. I inherited them. I did not select them.
  • 3.A real example beats a hypothetical, because vendors face this balance from the other side. Someone in a product meeting had to decide what to make easy. So do you.
  • 4.Every question I ask here, I would ask of any product in these categories, including the ones in your buildings.

Worked example · social studies

Traverse

Design choiceGive students the sources. Keep the interpretation.
The balanceRemoves an infrastructural barrier, which is archive access most schools never had. Leaves the noetic work intact, because someone still has to decide what a conflicting source is worth.
Stays yoursWhether your schedule gives students time to actually sit in the ambiguity, or whether the pacing guide quietly resolves it for them.

Worked example · mathematics

Imagine IM and ClassMate

Design choicePut the assistant where it touches planning, not learning. It sits inside the curriculum and faces the teacher.
The balanceReduces infrastructural load on the scarcest resource in any district, which is teacher time, without touching student cognition. This is the safest category of AI use in a school, and blanket caution often restricts it while permitting riskier things.
Stays yoursWhere your teachers draw the line between clerical help and instructional judgment. Assembling a warm-up is clerical. Choosing which misconception to target on Tuesday is judgment, and judgment is on the durable six.

Worked example · courseware

EdgeEX

Design choiceCompress feedback latency in asynchronous settings, where the realistic alternative is often no timely feedback at all.
The balanceTurnaround time is a genuine infrastructural barrier. A student who waits eight days for comments has effectively received none, because the moment for revision has passed. For alternative pathways this is real value, not a convenience.
Stays yoursWhat you do with the time it returns. Grading is also how a teacher discovers what a class does not understand. Redirect that time toward looking at student work and you have strengthened your practice. Absorb it into the schedule and you have quietly removed a teacher's main way of knowing a class.

Worked example · intervention

MyPath

Design choiceHold cognitive load in a band where a struggling student can actually engage.
The balanceA student working three grade levels below placement will not learn from grade-level text, and will not learn from repeated failure either. Reducing load enough to make engagement possible is the precondition for productive friction, not a bypass of it.
Stays yoursThe exit ramp. Scaffolds that never come down become ceilings, and an adaptive system optimizing for steady success has no internal reason to route a student toward difficulty. Deciding when the student meets the hard version is a district decision.

The pattern across all four

Every one of those ended in the same place.

The vendor decides what the tool makes easy. You decide what it means.

Two districts can run identical software and get opposite outcomes. That difference is not in the product. It is in whether anyone decided, on purpose, what to keep hard.

The Monday move · ninety seconds, silent

One competency. One protection. One owner.

Take your Portrait of a Graduate. Pick a single competency. Ask what in your current AI guidance protects it, and write down who is responsible for watching it.

Most of you will find nothing protecting it. That is the most useful thing you will learn today, and it is fixable by Friday.

Where we started

The districts that will be future ready are not the ones that adopted fastest.

They are the ones that decided, on purpose, what to keep hard.
